What should an external marketing function actually manage?

An accountable external marketing function owns the work between an approved priority and a measurable result: planning, production, approval, publishing, campaigns, follow-up and reporting.

12 August 20269 min readBy Run Lighter

Short answer

An external marketing function should manage the repeatable path from an approved priority to published work, active campaigns, followed-up enquiries and useful reporting. The business retains expert judgement, final approval, customer relationships and commercial decisions.

That distinction matters. Many businesses already have a strategy, several suppliers and enough software. What they do not have is one accountable operating system that turns priorities into completed work and shows what happened next.

A marketing plan is not a delivery system

A strategy can identify the right market, services and channels and still produce very little movement.

The problem usually appears after the plan is approved. Someone needs to turn each priority into a brief, find the source material, prepare the page or campaign, request approval, publish it, coordinate the next action and record the result. When responsibility is split across an owner, employees and several suppliers, the work slows down between them.

The owner becomes the unofficial project manager. They chase drafts, remember deadlines, answer supplier questions and try to join advertising, website, email and sales information after the fact.

An external marketing function should remove that coordination burden. It does not replace the people who understand the customers, services or professional obligations. It gives their decisions a reliable path into the market.

One accountable marketing work list

The first responsibility is not a social post or an advertisement. It is one visible work list.

Every active item should have a purpose, audience, owner, approval point, due date and next action. That includes website improvements, articles, newsletters, campaigns, professional outreach, client resources and reporting work.

This creates a simple distinction between work being prepared, work waiting for approval and work ready to publish. It also stops one supplier's task from disappearing inside another supplier's inbox.

A calendar shows when something is expected. A managed work list also shows what is preventing it from moving.

Paid campaigns and lead generation

Advertising management should not finish when somebody submits a form.

The visible campaign work includes the offer, audience, search terms, creative, budget and landing page. The operational work begins when a person responds. Their enquiry needs a known destination, a clear acknowledgement, an accountable owner and a visible next action.

A managed function should connect those parts. It should also distinguish between a click, a submitted form, a useful conversation, a customer and a confirmed commercial outcome. Those are different stages, and the report should not pretend they are interchangeable.

Automation can carry the repeated steps: recording the source, creating the lead, sending an acknowledgement, alerting the right person and creating a follow-up task. Qualification, advice, pricing and the sales conversation stay with the responsible person.

Website and local search

The website is where marketing promises, useful information and enquiry paths meet. Managing it involves more than occasional design changes.

An external function should maintain the website work list, coordinate technical suppliers and improve the pages most closely connected to the services the business wants to grow. That can include page structure, internal links, local relevance, titles, descriptions, landing pages and clear enquiry paths.

Existing work should be reviewed before it is replaced. If a current supplier, page or search position is performing a useful role, the goal is to preserve it. Changes should follow evidence rather than a general desire to make the site look newer.

Local visibility also needs to be considered alongside the website. Business information, service coverage, genuine reviews and the Google Business Profile should present one accurate picture of the organisation.

Expert-led article publishing

Professional knowledge should remain with the people qualified to provide it. The external function makes that knowledge easier to produce, approve, publish and reuse.

The process can begin with a practical topic and a clear writing brief. The appropriate expert prepares or approves the substance. The delivery team can then edit the approved material for clarity, structure, search visibility and channel fit, add the supporting image and links, and publish it on the website.

One useful article can support several pieces of work:

  • a website article or service-page improvement
  • a feature in a client newsletter
  • LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram content
  • a short frequently asked question
  • a Google Business Profile update
  • a section of a guide or brochure
  • a useful follow-up item for a professional contact

Repurposing is not copying the same paragraph everywhere. It is adapting one approved idea to the context in which someone will encounter it.

Social and email distribution

Publishing is only useful when it reaches the right audience consistently.

Social media should have a defined role within the wider programme. It may support credibility, make useful expertise visible, reinforce a campaign or give an existing contact another reason to return. Follower count alone does not establish whether paid advertising is working or whether the activity creates useful enquiries.

Email can serve several audiences with different needs. Existing customers may value practical updates. Professional contacts may need occasional, relevant material that helps them recognise when a referral is appropriate. Prospects may need a clear next step after requesting information.

The external function should manage audience groups, preparation, approval, testing, scheduling and link tracking. The business still owns the tone, professional boundaries and relationships.

Professional relationships and referral support

Important relationships should remain personal, but the work around them does not need to rely on memory.

A managed system can maintain agreed contact groups, prepare useful outreach, record replies, schedule follow-up and make the next action visible. It can help prepare meeting material and show which relationships have been active without pretending that a database has created trust.

Directors, owners and subject-matter experts still hold the conversations. The external function gives those conversations consistent support before and after they happen.

Guides, brochures and campaign material

Substantial resources often stall because no one owns the complete production path.

A client guide, professional resource, brochure or event invitation needs a purpose, approved source material, editing, design, production and distribution. Treating each as a separate creative project creates repeated briefing and approval work.

An external function should connect these materials to the wider calendar. A strong article series may become a guide. A guide may support a newsletter, campaign or professional conversation. The aim is to make approved expertise work harder without inventing new claims.

Lead tracking and a useful dashboard

A dashboard should answer commercial questions, not simply display every available number.

Website visits, impressions and clicks help diagnose activity. The more useful view connects them, where the information is available, to enquiries, conversations, customers and known revenue.

The limitation is important. No provider can create complete attribution when source information, account access or downstream outcomes are unavailable. A responsible dashboard makes the known path visible and identifies where the evidence stops.

The owner should be able to see:

  • what work was completed
  • what campaigns are active
  • what was spent
  • which enquiries were recorded
  • where those enquiries came from when known
  • who owns the next action
  • which outcomes are confirmed
  • what should be improved next

Reporting should help make the next decision. It should not manufacture certainty.

What stays with the business?

External does not mean unaccountable, and managed does not mean unsupervised.

The business should retain:

  • expert and professional judgement
  • factual and technical accuracy
  • final approval of public claims
  • advertising budget approval
  • qualification, advice and pricing decisions
  • ownership of customer and professional relationships
  • access to its website, advertising accounts, mailing lists, CRM and data

The external function can prepare, coordinate, publish, operate and measure the agreed work. It should also maintain a clear record of approvals, changes and responsibilities.

This human boundary is the same principle used when we automate the work around marketing: systems carry repeated movement, while people retain context and accountability.

What does a sensible operating rhythm look like?

The precise output depends on the organisation, but the rhythm should be visible before work begins.

Weekly work may include publishing approved material, improving a priority website page, optimising active advertising, maintaining social distribution, following up agreed professional activity and updating known enquiry results.

Monthly work may include a client newsletter, a targeted outreach activity and a recorded performance and planning review.

Quarterly work may include a substantial guide or resource and a review of priorities, campaigns and content performance.

The important part is not producing activity for the sake of a schedule. It is knowing what the service is responsible for, what it needs from the business and how delays or missing approvals will be handled.

How to assess an external marketing service

Before appointing a provider, ask five practical questions.

Who owns the complete path?

If website, advertising, content and reporting are split between several parties, identify who is responsible for the handoffs.

What will be completed regularly?

Ask for the operating rhythm and completed outputs, not only a list of available capabilities.

What still requires internal approval?

Clarify which decisions, source material and professional claims must come from the business.

Who owns the accounts and information?

The business should know where its website, campaigns, audiences, leads and performance records live and who can access them.

How will useful outcomes be recorded?

Define the difference between activity, an enquiry, a qualified conversation and a completed commercial outcome. Report only what can be verified.

An external function should make marketing easier to run

The value is not another stream of tasks for the owner to supervise. It is one accountable delivery path from agreed priority to completed work, human follow-up and evidence.

For some businesses, that requires a full external implementation function. For others, the immediate problem may be one broken handoff, an unclear work list or incomplete tracking. The right starting point depends on what is already working and where responsibility currently stops.
